# Circuits 04 — Towards Monosemanticity

**Towards Monosemanticity: Decomposing Language Models With Dictionary Learning** (Bricken, Templeton, Batson et al., Anthropic, October 2023) is the paper that turns the diagnosis of Presentation 03 into a cure. Because **superposition** packs more features than dimensions into a network, individual neurons are **polysemantic** and cannot be read one at a time. The authors train a **sparse autoencoder (SAE)** — an *overcomplete* encoder/decoder with an **L1 sparsity penalty** — on the MLP activations of a **one-layer transformer with 512 neurons**. This **dictionary learning** procedure recovers **more than 4,000 monosemantic, human-interpretable features** (up to tens of thousands in larger runs): clean detectors for **DNA, base64, Arabic, Hebrew and Korean script, HTTP requests, legal text, citations** and context-specific tokens. The features are shown to be real on three fronts — **specificity**, **causal effect on output logits** (activating the DNA feature promotes `A/C/G/T` tokens), and **automated interpretability** scoring — and they exhibit **feature splitting** (granularity scales with dictionary size) and **universality** across independently trained models. For an engineer, this is the first convincing proof that **superposition is resolvable**: a model's computation can be decomposed into a large, interpretable, causal feature dictionary — the proof-of-concept that scales up in Presentation 05.
